ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Moving from AI Interest
to Business Value

AI can create significant value, but only when it is connected to a real business problem. The opportunity is not simply to adopt AI. It is to use it where it can improve performance, productivity or decision-making.

Artificial intelligence has moved rapidly from experimentation into mainstream business discussions.

Yet many organisations still struggle to translate AI interest into measurable results. The technology is available. The challenge is deciding where it should actually be applied.

The principle

Start with the business problem, not the technology. The strongest AI opportunities are usually found where repetitive work, large volumes of information or complex decisions create measurable friction.

Where AI creates practical value

AI does not need to transform an entire organisation to generate value. Focused applications can produce meaningful improvements when they address a specific operational or commercial challenge.

Reduce repetitive work

Automate activities that consume significant amounts of time without creating equivalent business value.

Improve decision-making

Use data and AI-supported analysis to help teams identify patterns, risks and opportunities more quickly.

Improve customer experience

Use AI to make interactions faster, more relevant and easier to scale.

Increase organisational capacity

Give employees better tools to research, analyse, create and execute without simply adding more resources.

The real challenge is adoption

Selecting an AI tool is rarely the most difficult part. The harder question is how that tool becomes part of the way people actually work.

Without clear ownership, appropriate processes and employee adoption, even technically strong solutions can deliver limited value.

Technology alone is not transformation.

The value appears when technology, processes and people work together around a clearly defined business outcome.

A practical framework for AI adoption

Organisations can reduce the risk of AI initiatives by following a simple progression.

01

Identify

Find business processes where AI could realistically create measurable value.

02

Prioritise

Select opportunities based on business impact, feasibility and implementation risk.

03

Test

Start with a focused use case rather than attempting a large-scale transformation immediately.

04

Measure

Define clear measures for productivity, quality, cost, revenue or customer experience.

05

Scale

Expand successful applications only when the business case and operating model support it.

Start small. Think strategically.

A successful AI programme does not need dozens of use cases. A small number of well-selected initiatives can create more value than a large portfolio of disconnected experiments.

The objective should be to establish a repeatable approach for identifying, testing and scaling AI opportunities across the organisation.

Questions worth asking

  • Where are teams spending significant time on repetitive work?
  • Which decisions could benefit from better access to information?
  • Where is customer or employee experience being constrained?
  • Which AI opportunities could produce measurable value within months?
  • What would need to change for employees to adopt the solution?

Key takeaway

The organisations that benefit most from AI will not necessarily be those that adopt the most technology.

They will be the organisations that identify the right problems, connect AI to measurable outcomes and integrate it into the way work gets done.
