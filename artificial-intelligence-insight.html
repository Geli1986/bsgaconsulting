Artificial Intelligence: From Hype to Value

The question for organisations is no longer whether AI matters, but where it can create meaningful and measurable business value.

BSGA Insight Artificial Intelligence
Technology perspective
The BSGA View

Artificial Intelligence is moving rapidly from experimentation into everyday business. The organisations that benefit most will be those that connect AI adoption to real business priorities.

AI has moved quickly from a specialist technology discussion into a strategic business issue. Organisations are experimenting with generative AI, automation and intelligent tools across an increasingly broad range of activities.

Yet experimentation does not automatically create value. The challenge is to identify where AI can improve performance, integrate it into real workflows and create the organisational conditions required for adoption.

Start with the business problem.

One of the biggest risks of AI adoption is starting with the technology rather than the problem.

New tools can be impressive, but their value depends on whether they address a meaningful business need. The strongest use cases typically connect AI directly to a measurable improvement in productivity, customer experience, decision quality or operational performance.

This changes the conversation from "What can AI do?" to a more useful question: "Where could AI materially improve the way our organisation works?"

01

Prioritise

Focus on use cases where potential business impact, feasibility and organisational readiness come together.

02

Experiment

Test high-value opportunities quickly and learn what works before committing significant resources.

03

Adopt

Move successful experiments into real workflows, connecting AI capabilities with the people and processes that use them.

04

Govern

Establish appropriate controls around data, risk, accountability and responsible use as AI becomes part of everyday operations.

AI needs adoption.

Technology alone does not transform an organisation. The value of an AI solution depends on whether people actually use it and whether it becomes part of the way work is performed.

This makes adoption a critical part of any AI strategy. Employees need to understand what is changing, why it matters and how the new technology affects their responsibilities.

Organisations also need to consider how processes, roles and management practices evolve as AI becomes embedded into the operating model.

From experimentation to business value.

The next stage of AI adoption is not about pursuing more experiments. It is about becoming more selective about where AI is applied and more disciplined about measuring the value it creates.

This requires a clear connection between technology decisions and business priorities. AI initiatives should have a defined purpose, an identifiable owner and a meaningful way of evaluating impact.

Organisations that approach AI in this way are better positioned to move beyond isolated demonstrations and create capabilities that contribute to sustained business performance.

Key Takeaway

AI creates value when technology, people and business priorities move together.
