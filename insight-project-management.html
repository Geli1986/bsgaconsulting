PROJECT MANAGEMENT BSGA INSIGHT

Why projects fail before they start.

Project delivery problems are often created long before the first milestone is missed. The foundations matter.

PROJECT MANAGEMENT 5 MIN READ

When a project goes wrong, attention usually turns to execution. But many project failures are already embedded in the way the project was defined.

Unclear objectives, competing priorities, weak ownership and unrealistic expectations create problems that become increasingly expensive to fix once delivery is underway.

The real problem is often clarity.

A project can have a strong team, a detailed plan and sophisticated reporting and still struggle if nobody has a shared understanding of what success actually means.

01

Unclear outcomes

Activities become the focus instead of the business outcome the project is expected to create.

02

Weak ownership

When accountability is shared too widely, important decisions can remain unresolved.

03

Too many priorities

Projects rarely fail because teams have too little work. More often, they have too many competing demands.

04

Governance without decisions

Meetings and reporting create little value if they do not lead to timely decisions, escalation or action.

Start with the outcome.

Before building a detailed project plan, define the outcome the organisation actually needs. What will be different when the project succeeds?

That question creates a stronger foundation for scope, priorities, governance, resources and measurement.

It also makes difficult decisions easier. When priorities compete, the desired outcome provides a reference point for deciding what matters most.

Good governance should enable delivery.

Governance is not about adding layers of meetings. It is about creating clarity around who decides, who owns delivery and when issues need to be escalated.

The most effective project environments tend to make three things very clear: ownership, decision rights and accountability.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Strong project delivery starts before execution. Define the outcome, establish ownership and create the conditions for decisions to happen quickly.
