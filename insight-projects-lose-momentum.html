01 PROJECTS

Why projects lose momentum.

The practical signals that a project is starting to drift — and what leaders can do before performance deteriorates.

PROJECT MANAGEMENT BSGA INSIGHT

Projects rarely fail suddenly. More often, momentum is gradually lost.

Priorities become less clear. Decisions take longer. Ownership becomes blurred. Meetings increase while progress slows.

By the time the problem becomes visible in a project report, the underlying issue may already have been developing for weeks.

The warning signs are usually practical.

The most useful project controls are not necessarily the most sophisticated ones. Strong delivery often depends on recognising a small number of signals early.

01

Decisions are taking longer.

When key decisions remain open, teams begin working around uncertainty. This creates rework and gradually reduces delivery speed.

02

Priorities keep changing.

A project can absorb change, but constant reprioritisation makes accountability difficult and weakens focus.

03

Meetings replace progress.

An increasing number of meetings can be a sign that ownership, decisions or escalation routes are unclear.

04

Accountability becomes unclear.

When responsibilities are shared without clear ownership, problems can remain visible without anyone being responsible for resolving them.

Momentum comes from clarity.

Effective project management is not simply about tracking tasks. It is about creating the conditions in which people can make decisions, understand priorities and act with accountability.

That requires clear governance, realistic priorities, visible ownership and a delivery rhythm that keeps attention focused on outcomes.

THE TAKEAWAY

When a project starts losing momentum, the answer is rarely more reporting. Start by restoring clarity around priorities, decisions and ownership.
